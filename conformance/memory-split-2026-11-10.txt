What 40,000 element sets hold in GPKit, split: the propagators themselves against the input text and the records read
from it. It splits the 174 MB measured at 40,000 sets in benchmark-stream-2026-11-10.txt (the owner's ruling of
2026-11-10: measure first; propose a design only if the propagators dominate).
Run on 2026-11-10 (UTC), 16:55:27Z to 16:57Z, on a Mac17,4 (Apple M5, 16 GB), macOS 27.2.1 (26B131), Apple Swift
version 6.4.2, release build, branch v2-propagator-memory (GPKit 1.3.0 with v2-streaming). Each stage ran in a process of
its own, started only with the one-minute load at 12 or less, no xcodebuild and no simulator round. The load was
10.8 to 11.2. At 40,000 every stage ran twice; the runs agree to 0.1 MB except "all", 173.6 and 175.6 MB at the peak.

How it was run, one process a stage:

  swift run -c release --package-path tools/benchmark benchmark --count 40000 --memory STAGE

STAGE is one of:
- none: nothing made;
- text: the CSV text;
- file: the text and the file GPKit reads from it;
- all: the text, the file and the propagators, as the other benchmark modes keep them;
- sets-only: the element sets alone, read 1,000 at a time, so that no whole text or file is ever held;
- propagators-only: the propagators alone, made the same way.

"Most memory held" is the process's resident peak. "Footprint" is Darwin's phys_footprint at the end, with
everything the stage made still held: what the system counts against an app. The sets are the tool's made-up
constellation. A real provider file's names and keywords would differ in size per set [untested].

Sizes the compiler gives: Propagator 1,368 bytes, of which its ElementSet is 472 and the SGP4 state and gravity 896
(a Propagator holds its ElementSet as a value).

The split of the 174 MB at 40,000 sets (footprints less the 2.3 MB of an empty process; "all" is 145.2 MB of
footprint, its 174 MB peak including what parsing holds while it runs):

- the propagators, with the element sets they hold: 79.4 MB, 55 per cent. Of that:
  - the Propagator array, 52.2 MB: 35.8 MB of SGP4 state and 16.4 MB of element sets held inline;
  - the element sets' own allocations, about 27 MB (sets-only's 45.1 MB less its 18.0 MB array), about 690 bytes a
    set. That figure includes what the allocator kept from reading 1,000 at a time [inferred: not split further].
- the input text, 13.6 MB, 9 per cent.
- the file read from it, the records and their own copies of the element sets: 79 MB of "file" less 13.6 MB of
  text; on top of the propagators in "all", 52 MB. That is 36 per cent.

Linear: the propagators alone are 22.3 MB at 10,000 sets and 79.4 MB at 40,000, 2.2 and 2.0 KB a set.

The verdict, against the owner's test:
- The propagators are the largest part, 55 per cent of what "all" holds. They do not dominate it: the input text and
  the file are the other 45 per cent.
- That part goes away without any change to GPKit, by reading in pieces and keeping only the propagators, as
  propagators-only does: 87 MB at the peak against 174.
- So no design is proposed (the owner's rule).
- What remains, if a phone needs less than 2 KB a set: the 896 bytes of SGP4 state, and the element set each
  propagator keeps (472 bytes inline and about 690 of its own). That is a question for the owner, not a design here.
